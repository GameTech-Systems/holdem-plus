Hold’em Plus Strategy Guide

Texas Hold’em with one extra hole card, dealt after the flop. Everything else still applies, but best-5-of-8 changes which hands are worth playing and how strong a “good” hand really is.

What the extra card does: it pulls every hand toward average. Heads-up, AA falls from 85% to 75% while 72o rises from 35% to 40%. Against a full table, premium pairs lose the most (AA 49% to 35% against five hands). Suited and connected hands gain the most relative to Hold’em (87s: 48% to 53% heads-up), and big offsuit hands lose (A9o: 61% to 54%).

Core principles

  • The flop is the real decision street. It is the first betting round where every player has all 3 cards.
  • Think nuts, not “good hand.” With 8 cards to choose from, second-best flushes and straights lose more often than they do in Hold’em.
  • One pair is weaker. Top pair with a weak kicker is a bluff-catcher; fold it to large river bets more than you would in Hold’em.
  • Charge the draws. More outs and combo draws mean bigger bets on wet boards, and more semi-bluffs when you hold the draw.
  • Watch the board. Paired boards make full houses and quads more likely, and a flush or straight can be beaten by a hand you did not consider.
  • Pot odds are unchanged. The rule of 2 and 4 still estimates your equity; just count the extra outs your 3rd card gives.
  • Rabbit Runner is information, not value. It cannot change the hand you folded. Treat it as a paid extra for curiosity or learning. In tournaments the fee is cash or e-notes on DAS, never tournament chips.

Ring game vs tournament

Ring game (cash)

  • Every chip is worth its face value, so take any +EV risk, including all-ins.
  • Deep stacks reward implied odds: suited aces, suited connectors and small pairs gain value. Set-mine when stacks are about 15–20 times the raise.
  • Position is your main edge. Open wider on the button and cutoff, much tighter from early seats.
  • Thin value bets with one pair lose value, since opponents’ calling ranges beat you more often.
  • Pick tables with loose, passive, multiway pots. Your strong hands get paid.
  • Rebuy freely when short. There is no survival value to protect.

Tournament

  • Chips lost cost more than chips won gain (ICM). Avoid marginal all-ins near the bubble and pay jumps; chip leaders should apply pressure.
  • Depth zones: above 30 big blinds, play normally. 15–30: drop speculative hands, raise or fold. 10–15: shove or fold. Under 10: push or fold only.
  • Blinds rise on a clock (the demo starts at 1/2 with 40 chips and 15-minute levels), so stacks shrink fast. Steal blinds early.
  • Heads-up, the button is the small blind and acts first preflop. Play wide: any ace, any pair, suited and broadway cards.
  • Premium edges shrink: AA is a 75% favorite heads-up here against 85% in Hold’em, so skip coin-flip all-ins you do not need.
Every number here is reproducible: simulation code, workflow and replication notes on GitHub. Equities are Monte Carlo results (300,000 deals heads-up, 150,000 against five hands; about ±0.1 points) against random opponents who never fold, with ties split. Hold’em and Plus use the same deals, so differences are exact comparisons. Equity against random hands is raw strength, not EV against real opponents who fold, so use it to rank hands, not to size bets. All-in EV per chip staked is players × equity − 1. Tiers and ranges are rule-of-thumb guidance informed by these numbers, not solver output. Odds on the three-card tab are exact draws from the 47 unseen cards after the flop.
