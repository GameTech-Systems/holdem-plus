## What this means in practice

You're free to:

- Fork this repository, modify it, and run it — including for a
  commercial product — under a different name.
- Describe your product as "based on" or "compatible with" the
  Hold'em Plus rules, in a purely descriptive, non-branding sense
  (nominative fair use), the same way it's fine to say a product
  "supports the MP3 format" without implying endorsement.

You need separate written permission to:

- Call your deployment, table, tournament, or product "Hold'em Plus,"
  "Dealer Assist Tech," or "Billion Dollar Challenge," or use their
  logos, in a way that suggests it's an official or sanctioned
  implementation.
- Use "Billion Dollar Challenge" tier names, ladder structure, or
  qualification pathways as an officially sanctioned event. Official
  status is tied to the sanctioned network and certified Dealer Assist
  hardware, not merely to running this code.
- Use "GameTech Systems" or the Rights Holder's name to imply
  affiliation, endorsement, or certification you don't have.

## Why this is separate from the code license

Software copyright protects the literal code — not game rules,
tournament formats, or brand names. Keeping the code permissively
licensed and open lets other operators and developers adopt the
*rules engine* with minimal friction, while trademark protection keeps
control over who can market something as an official Hold'em Plus /
Billion Dollar Challenge product. These are intentionally two separate
mechanisms, not one license doing both jobs.
